Konnor’s Online Statistics Notes

“It’s easy to lie with statistics, it’s hard to tell the truth without it.” — Andrejs Dunkels

These Notes: Questions and Answers
  • What? A unified, rigorous, yet compact introduction to statistical science, from elementary set theory and probability and eventually to causal inference, empirical processes, and semiparametric models
  • Who? Primarily undergraduate and early graduate students in statistics, mathematics, and other quantitative disciplines who have some previous exposure to sets, basic proof logic and who are interested in understanding what statistics looks like
  • Why? To help readers develop the reasoning needed to evaluate claims, learn from evidence, and be meaningfully data-driven
  • Where? Here
  • When? Every day. Repetition is the mother of learning
  • How? By showing how a relatively small collection of ideas ties statistics together, without sacrificing mathematical rigor or the intuition that comes from exploring those ideas interactively
Why I Wrote These Notes

Statistics is a rich and relatively young field, one that you, too, can be part of. These notes are my attempt to write the resource I wish I’d had as a young man searching for my own purpose.

Even if I don’t convince you to become a statistician, I hope you’ll see how much light statistical thinking can shed on the questions we all face amid the deluge of data in this demon haunted world. Wherever you come from and wherever you’re headed, I hope you’ll get in the habit of asking how much of that story the evidence actually supports, whether you welcome the conclusion or find it hard to accept.

These notes are my ongoing effort to succinctly organize and develop the mathematical and statistical ideas I’ve encountered and found useful in my studies, research, and reading across applied fields. My goal is to build a growing collection of interactive notes, progressing from tried-and-true mathematical foundations to core statistical methods and more advanced theory.

Throughout, I aim to emphasize not just how methods work, but why they work, what assumptions they rely on, and how they connect to problems in practice. Wherever possible, I’ll incorporate examples, visualizations, and interactive tools to make abstract ideas more intuitive.

To get started, I’ve put together two short introductions that provide some motivation and a broader perspective on the material:

  • Core Questions
  • A Short Overview

The notes are organized into three broad sections, progressing from foundational mathematics through core statistical ideas to more advanced topics. This is very much a work in progress! I’m currently developing the foundational sections, while the remaining topics reflect what I hope to cover over time. Ultimately, I hope this collection will help others see how the different pieces of statistics fit together and develop a better understanding of the bigger picture.

The organization and framing of these notes are heavily influenced by the causal roadmap and, more generally, by the distinction between what we want to learn from data (the estimand) and how we use data to estimate it (the estimator). I also used several blogs and online resources,

  • Statistics Education and Interactive Learning Resources There are already many excellent online resources for learning different parts of statistics, arguably better than my own in their respective areas, including:

    • Seeing Theory — Brown University. Really fun interactive visualizations of fundamental statistical concepts. I think this is a great starting point for complete beginners.

  • StatQuest with Josh Starmer. Accessible and entertaining explanations through videos and songs.

  • Harvard Statistics 110 — Joseph Blitzstein. A wildly popular and wonderfully taught introduction to probability. I think my introductory Thinking Statistically handout falls somewhere between Starmer’s intuitive explanations and Blitzstein’s more trasitionally mathematical approach, with a greater emphasis on how statistical ideas fit into scientific reasoning.
